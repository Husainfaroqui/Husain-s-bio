Cell Membrane

The plasma membrane is a selectively permeable bilayer separating the cell's interior from its environment. It is composed of phospholipids, proteins, and cholesterol.

Each phospholipid has a hydrophilic head and two hydrophobic fatty acid tails. The bilayer arranges so heads face outward while tails form a hydrophobic core — a fluid mosaic model.

  • Phospholipid bilayer forms the structural backbone
  • Integral proteins span the full membrane thickness
  • Peripheral proteins attach to one surface only
  • Cholesterol stabilizes membrane fluidity
  • Selective permeability controls what enters and exits
  • Glycoproteins serve as cell-identity and signaling markers

Mitochondria

Mitochondria are the energy-producing organelles of the cell, generating ATP through cellular respiration. They contain their own circular DNA, supporting the endosymbiotic theory of their origin.

The inner membrane is extensively folded into cristae, increasing surface area for oxidative phosphorylation where ATP synthase produces ATP.

  • Outer membrane: smooth and relatively permeable
  • Inner membrane: folded into cristae
  • Matrix contains enzymes, mtDNA and ribosomes
  • ATP synthase acts as a molecular motor
  • Proton gradients drive ATP production
  • Mitochondria play a central role in cellular respiration

Golgi Apparatus

The Golgi apparatus is the cell's processing and distribution center. It receives proteins and lipids from the endoplasmic reticulum, modifies them, sorts them and sends them toward their destinations.

It consists of flattened cisternae stacked together. The cis face receives incoming material while the trans face dispatches processed material.

  • Receives proteins and lipids from the rough ER
  • Modifies carbohydrate chains
  • Processes proteins and lipids
  • Sorts cargo according to destination
  • Helps form lysosomes
  • Important for secretion of cellular products
